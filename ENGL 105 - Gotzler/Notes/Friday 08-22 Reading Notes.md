## Passage 1 Notes - Digital Humanities: Values & Methods

### Critical & Theoretical

- Grounded in theory and traditional scholarly criticism
- **Self-critical** of own tools, technologies, and platforms

### Iterative & Experimental

- Embraces **versioning and experimentation**
- Encourages risk-taking, redefinition, and failure
- Resists premature standardization

### Collaborative & Distributed

- **Multiple authors** and cross-disciplinary teams
- Networks include: scholars, students, programmers, librarians, designers

### Multimodal & Performative

- Beyond print constraints
- Uses **text, audio, video** and multiple modes
- **Performative texts** that break reader/writer dichotomy

### Open & Accessible

- Embraces **open access** and public publishing
- Creative Commons licensing
- Pre/post-publication peer review via social media

## Key Methods

### Content & Text Analysis

- **Enhanced Critical Curation** - object-based arguments through digital media
- **Augmented Editions** - marked up texts, crowd-sourced, open to revision
- **Fluid Textuality** - perpetual annotation and remix

### Scale & Data

- **Law of Large Numbers** - computational analysis of massive datasets
- **Distant/Close Reading** - analyzing large corpora for patterns vs. traditional close reading
- **Cultural Analytics** - mining and visualizing cultural materials

### Visualization & Space

- **Data Design** - arguments through data visualization
- **Locative Investigation** - "thick mapping" and GIS
- **Virtual/Spatial Representations** - geo-referencing and simulated environments

### Interactive & Dynamic

- **Animated Archive** - making static archives experiential
- **Humanities Gaming** - historical simulation and interactive narratives
- **Database Documentaries** - multi-path, user-explored narratives

### Technical & Platform Studies

- **Code Studies** - analyzing programmer-written code as text
- **Software Studies** - examining tools and platforms
- **Platform Studies** - understanding social/cultural platform interactions

### Collaborative Production

- **Distributed Knowledge Production** - challenging traditional authorship
- **Performative Access** - user-based performance of texts
- **Crowdsourcing** - collective content creation

### Digital Culture

- **Remix Culture** - sample, migrate, translate, reuse digital objects
- **Repurposable Content** - all digital objects subject to critical reuse
- **Ubiquitous Scholarship** - beyond print, multiple publishing modes
- **Pervasive Infrastructure** - cloud storage, distributed servers, multiple screens

# Digital Humanities: From Humanities to Digital Humanities

## Core Definition

**Digital Humanities** is born from the encounter between traditional humanities and computational methods. It represents the migration of cultural materials into networked environments, creating new challenges and opportunities for humanistic inquiry.

## Key Characteristics

- **Collaborative and generative** (vs. traditional solitary scholarship)
- **Production-based** - emphasis on making things while studying
- **Iterative and experimental** - embraces versioning and productive failure
- **Transmedia** - works across multiple platforms and media formats

## Historical Development

### From Humanism to Modern Humanities

- **Renaissance origins**: Shift from medieval theocratic worldview to human-centered perspective
- **Editorial foundations**: Recovery and transcription of classical texts shaped humanistic methods
- **Print culture**: Standardization and dissemination of cultural materials (printing press as analog to digital transformation)
- **19th-20th century**: Disciplinary specialization and institutionalization

### Digital Transformation Waves

1. **First wave (1940s-1980s)**: Focus on corpus building, text encoding, database creation
2. **Web era (1990s)**: Shift from processing to networking, need for standards
3. **Contemporary era**: Visualization, geospatial analysis, multimedia approaches

## Transmedia Modes of Argumentation

### Beyond Print-Based Scholarship

- Digital environments require new forms, tools, and schemata
- **21st century communication**: Scalable databases, visualizations, video, multiuser platforms
- **Additive pedagogy**: Students learn both traditional writing and new digital literacies
- **Multum in parvo**: Ability to distill complex ideas into compact, networked forms

### Visual and Oral Renaissance

- Screen culture makes visual elements fundamental to Digital Humanities
- **Orality returns**: YouTube lectures, podcasts, "demo culture"
- **Design becomes argument**: Visual rhetoric carries intellectual content
- Integration of sound, motion graphics, animation, video

## Design as Intellectual Method

### Design Principles

- **Propositional orientation**: "What if?" and "What happens when...?"
- **Collaborative requirement**: Humanists + designers + technologists
- **Argument embodiment**: Design choices express theoretical positions

### Historical Precedents

- Marshall McLuhan's "The Medium is the Massage"
- John Berger's "Ways of Seeing"
- Scott McCloud's "Understanding Comics"

## Computational Activities

### Foundation Layer: Computation

- **Challenge**: Computation requires disambiguation; humanities embrace ambiguity
- **Mutual influence**: Humanistic approaches alter computational methods
- **Key tension**: How to embody interpretation, contingency, positionality in computation

### Processing Activities

- Sorting, searching, calculating, matching
- **Markup languages**: Insert interpretation into digital text streams
- **Structured data**: Enable analysis and display

### Basic Building Blocks

- Digitization
- Classification
- Description and metadata
- Organization
- Navigation

## Four Core Activities

### 1. Curation

- Selection and organization of materials in interpretive frameworks
- Enhanced forms show artifacts shaped by networks of influence
- Goes beyond traditional museum/library approaches

### 2. Analysis

- **Close reading**: Stylometrics, genre analysis, author attribution
- **Distant reading**: Large-scale data processing
- **Visualization**: Making analytical results graphically legible
- Emerging focus on geo-temporal mapping

### 3. Editing

- Revived and expanded in digital environments
- **Productive and generative**: Creative activity of making
- **Genetic editions**: Incorporating variants, versions, amendments
- Foundation for all interpretive work

### 4. Modeling

- **Content models**: Shapes of argument expressed in information structures
- Every digital project embodies assumptions about knowledge
- **Interface design**: May differ from underlying data organization
- **3D/Virtual environments**: Immersive historical reconstructions

## Generative Humanities Concept

### Core Principles

- **Process over product**
- **Versioning over definitive editions**
- **Experimentation and risk-taking**
- **Productive failure** as learning mechanism

### Prototyping Culture

- Rapid creation, testing, reworking
- **Iterative versioning** fosters experimentation
- Distributed testing with users and developers
- **Failure as necessity**: "It is not an experiment if it cannot fail"

### Collaborative Framework

- Team-based approaches challenge traditional single-author model
- Need for new credit systems for intellectual labor
- **Polymaths vs. specialists**: Room for both approaches
- Technical imagination + discipline-specific knowledge

## Digital Humanities as Core Curriculum

### Arguments For

- **Generative approach**: Making while learning
- **Process-focused**: Emphasis on methods over content
- **Multimodal literacy**: Games, video, exhibits as pedagogical tools
- **21st century skills**: Critical design, speculative imagination

### Addressing Traditional Concerns

- **Not replacement** but extension of traditional humanities methods
- **Object-agnostic**: Works with ancient pottery shards or contemporary videos
- **Democratic potential**: Expands constituency beyond academic specialists
- **Practical relevance**: Prepares students for networked, globalized world

## Infrastructure and Sustainability

### Technical Requirements

- Servers, software, systems administration
- Compatibility and interoperability
- Network infrastructure

### Cultural Dimensions

- Museums, libraries, archives as partners
- International collaboration challenges
- Long-term preservation and access issues

## Critical Considerations

### Potential Pitfalls

- **Neo-liberal speedup**: Demanding more skills without compensation
- **Premature standardization**: Risk of limiting experimentation
- **Disciplinary colonization**: Risk of Digital Humanities becoming catch-all
- **Deliverable-focused metrics**: May conflict with research innovation

### Success Factors

- Maintain experimental ethos
- Support risk-taking and failure
- Develop appropriate assessment methods
- Balance normalization with innovation

## Future Vision

Digital Humanities as expanded mode of humanistic inquiry that:

- Preserves traditional interpretive skills
- Adds computational and design capabilities
- Engages broader public audiences
- Creates new forms of knowledge production
- Prepares students for 21st century challenges

The field represents a "once-in-a-generation moment" to reinvigorate humanities education and research through generative, collaborative, and experimental approaches while maintaining core humanistic values of interpretation, critical thinking, and cultural understanding.
