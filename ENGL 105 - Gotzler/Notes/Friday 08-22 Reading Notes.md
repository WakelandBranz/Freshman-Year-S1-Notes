## Passage 1 Notes - Digital Humanities: Values & Methods

### Critical & Theoretical

- Grounded in theory and traditional scholarly criticism
- **Self-critical** of own tools, technologies, and platforms

### Iterative & Experimental

- Embraces **versioning and experimentation**
- Encourages risk-taking, redefinition, and failure
- Resists premature standardization

### Collaborative & Distributed

- **Multiple authors** and cross-disciplinary teams
- Networks include: scholars, students, programmers, librarians, designers

### Multimodal & Performative

- Beyond print constraints
- Uses **text, audio, video** and multiple modes
- **Performative texts** that break reader/writer dichotomy

### Open & Accessible

- Embraces **open access** and public publishing
- Creative Commons licensing
- Pre/post-publication peer review via social media

## Key Methods

### Content & Text Analysis

- **Enhanced Critical Curation** - object-based arguments through digital media
- **Augmented Editions** - marked up texts, crowd-sourced, open to revision
- **Fluid Textuality** - perpetual annotation and remix

### Scale & Data

- **Law of Large Numbers** - computational analysis of massive datasets
- **Distant/Close Reading** - analyzing large corpora for patterns vs. traditional close reading
- **Cultural Analytics** - mining and visualizing cultural materials

### Visualization & Space

- **Data Design** - arguments through data visualization
- **Locative Investigation** - "thick mapping" and GIS
- **Virtual/Spatial Representations** - geo-referencing and simulated environments

### Interactive & Dynamic

- **Animated Archive** - making static archives experiential
- **Humanities Gaming** - historical simulation and interactive narratives
- **Database Documentaries** - multi-path, user-explored narratives

### Technical & Platform Studies

- **Code Studies** - analyzing programmer-written code as text
- **Software Studies** - examining tools and platforms
- **Platform Studies** - understanding social/cultural platform interactions

### Collaborative Production

- **Distributed Knowledge Production** - challenging traditional authorship
- **Performative Access** - user-based performance of texts
- **Crowdsourcing** - collective content creation

### Digital Culture

- **Remix Culture** - sample, migrate, translate, reuse digital objects
- **Repurposable Content** - all digital objects subject to critical reuse
- **Ubiquitous Scholarship** - beyond print, multiple publishing modes
- **Pervasive Infrastructure** - cloud storage, distributed servers, multiple screens
