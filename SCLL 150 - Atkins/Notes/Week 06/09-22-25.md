### Articles of Confederation
##### Weaknesses
 - **No power to tax** – The federal government couldn't collect taxes, relying on states to contribute funds voluntarily.
- **No national army** – There was no standing army to defend the country or handle internal unrest.
- **Weak central government** – Most power was held by the states, making national coordination difficult.
- **No executive branch** – There was no president or central leadership to enforce laws.
- **Unanimous consent for amendments** – All 13 states had to agree to amend the Articles, making change nearly impossible.

**The constitution was signed on September 17th**

#### The Federalists
 - **Strong central government** – Federalists believed a powerful national government was necessary for unity and stability.
- **Support for the Constitution** – They backed the new Constitution as a replacement for the weak Articles of Confederation.
- **Checks and balances** – They believed the Constitution’s system of separated powers would prevent tyranny.
- **National economic policy** – Many supported a national bank, uniform currency, and federal control over trade.
- **Standing army and national defense** – Federalists argued a strong military was needed to protect the country from foreign and domestic threats.
 - They were also the first political party

#### The Antifederalists
 - **Stronger state governments** – Many Anti-Federalists wanted states to retain most political power, fearing a dominant national government.
- **A Bill of Rights** – They demanded clear protections for individual liberties before agreeing to the Constitution.
- **Opposition to a standing army** – Some feared a permanent national army could be used to oppress the people.
- **Local control and direct democracy** – Many supported small, localized republics where citizens could more directly influence government.
- **Suspicion of executive power** – They worried a strong president could become too much like a king.
