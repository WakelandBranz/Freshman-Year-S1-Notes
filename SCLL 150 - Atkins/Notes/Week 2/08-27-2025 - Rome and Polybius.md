#### Roman Government Structure
**Roman political evolution – concise outline**

1. **Monarchy (c. 753‑509 BCE)**
   - Seven kings; king held executive, religious & judicial power.
2. **Republic (509 BCE)**
   - Power shared by *consuls* (executive), *senate* (aristocratic), and *popular assemblies* (democratic).  
   - Early **separation of powers**.
3. **Polybius (c. 200‑118 BCE)**
   - Described Rome’s mixed constitution; did **not** create it.  
   - Highlighted the three‑fold blend of monarchy, aristocracy, democracy.
4. **Cyclical constitutional theory**
   - Polybius’ model: monarchy → aristocracy → democracy → tyranny → return to monarchy (anacyclosis).  
   - Rome’s shift from Republic to Empire fits this pattern.
5. **Mixed constitution of the Republic**
   - *Monarchical*: two consuls with imperium.  
   - *Aristocratic*: Senate controlling finance, foreign policy.  
   - *Democratic*: assemblies electing magistrates & passing laws.
6. **Augustus (27 BCE‑14 CE) – Principate**
   - Adopted title “Princeps” while keeping republican façade.  
   - **Gradual power creep**: controlled army, provinces, legislation via legal reforms & propaganda.
7. **Empire/Monarchy**
   - “Emperor” (*Imperator*) replaces “Princeps”; authority becomes hereditary.  
   - Senate remains advisory.
8. **Stability through late antiquity**
   - *Pax Romana* (1st‑2nd c.) → Diocletian’s reforms (284‑305 CE).  
   - Eastern Empire stays stable into the 6th century CE.
9. **212 CE – *Constitutio Antoniniana***
   - Caracalla grants Roman citizenship to all free subjects → ~13 million citizens.  
   - Expands tax base & unifies legal status.

#### The 7 Primary Constitutions Thought of by Polybius
- **Monarchy** – rule by one person.
- **Aristocracy** – rule by a few noble families.
- **Democracy** – rule by the many (the people).
- **Tyranny** – corrupt monarchy.
- **Oligarchy** – corrupt aristocracy.
- **Ochlocracy** – mob rule (corrupt democracy).
- **Mixed constitution** – balanced combination of the three good forms (the ideal state).

***Polybius states that as time goes on in a Monarchy it slowly turns into a tyranny as degeneracy is passed down and amplified from generation to generation.***

**Polybius’s cycle of constitutional degeneration (anacyclosis)**

- **Monarchy → Tyranny**  
  - Wise king → accumulates personal power → loses restraint → becomes oppressive.
- **Tyranny → Aristocracy**  
  - Tyrant’s excesses spark revolt.  
  - Wealthy, respected families lead the uprising, remove the tyrant, and assume control as the “best men.”
- **Aristocracy → Oligarchy**  
  - Noble few initially govern for the common good → gradually pursue their own wealth and privileges → power concentrates in a narrow elite.
- **Oligarchy → Democracy**  
  - Elite’s corruption alienates the masses.  
  - Popular pressure forces the oligarchs to share power with broader citizen assemblies.
- **Democracy → Ochlocracy (mob rule)**  
  - Free citizens participate, but unchecked passions and demagogues dominate → decisions driven by emotion, not reason.
- **Ochlocracy → Monarchy**  
  - Chaos and instability prompt the populace to demand a strong single leader → a new monarch is installed, restarting the cycle.

### Polybius' Praise of the Roman Constitution's Checks and Balances
- **Mixed constitution** – combines monarchy (consuls), aristocracy (senate), and democracy (popular assemblies) in a single state.  
- **Separation of powers** – no single office can dominate: consuls hold imperium, the senate controls finance & foreign policy, assemblies pass laws and elect magistrates.  
- **Mutual veto** – each branch can block actions of the others (e.g., the senate can refuse funding, assemblies can reject proposals, consuls can veto legislation).  
- **Collegiality** – two consuls share executive authority, preventing unilateral decisions.  
- **Term limits & accountability** – annual magistrate terms and the right of *provocatio* (appeal to the people) keep officials answerable.  
- **Broad participation** – citizens retain a voice through assemblies, limiting aristocratic dominance.  
- **Stability through balance** – the constant tension among the three elements averts the degeneration into tyranny, oligarchy, or mob rule.
