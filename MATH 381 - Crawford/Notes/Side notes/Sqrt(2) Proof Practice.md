### $\sqrt2$ is irrational

Assume, for contradiction, that $\sqrt2$ is rational.
Since $\sqrt2$ is rational, $\exists a,b \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $b \neq 0$ and no common factors other than 1 such that $\sqrt2 = \frac{a}{b}$.
Squaring both sides of the equation we get $2 = \frac{a^2}{b^2}$.
Multiplying both sides of the equation by $b^2$ we get $2b^2 = a^2$.
This means that $a^2$ is even which implies that $a$ is even.
Since $a$ is even, $\exists k \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $a = 2k$.
Replacing $a$ with $2k$ we get $2b^2 = (2k)^2 = 4k^2$. 
Dividing both sides of the equation by 2 we get $b^2 = 2k^2$.
This means that $b^2$ is also even which implies that $b$ is even.
Both $a$ and $b$ being even contradicts our prior statement that $a$ and $b$ shared no common factors other than 1. 
This contradicts our original assumption  that $\sqrt2$ is rational, so $\sqrt2$ must be rational.
