## Definitions and Examples

### Subject and Predicate

**Definition:** Subject and Predicate

**Example 1:** Determine the subject and predicate of the statement x > 3.

- **Subject** - x
- **Predicate** - is greater than 3 (> 3) 

### Propositional Function

**Definition:** Propositional Function

**Example 2:** Let Q(x, y) be "x + y = 3". Determine the truth value of Q(3, 0) and Q(3, 2).

- Q(3, 0) - True... Q(3, 0) == 3 + 0 = 3
- Q(3, 2) - False... Q(3, 2) == 3 + 2 != 3

**Example 3:** Let A(s, m) be "Student s is a m major." If James is only a math major and Barb is only a biology major, determine the truth value of A(James, biology) and A(Barb, biology).

### Universal Quantification

**Definition:** Universal Quantification - ***Tells us that a predicate is true for every element under consideration***

**Mathematics Symbols we will be using:**

**Example 4:** Determine the truth value of ∀xP(x) where the domain is ℝ and P(x) is the statement x + 1 > x.

**Example 5:** Let P(x) be x² > 0. Determine the truth value of the statement ∀xP(x) where the domain is ℤ.

**Example 6:** Determine the truth value of the statement ∀x (x² ≥ x) where the domain is ℝ; ℤ.

### Existential Quantification

**Definition:** Existential Quantification - ***Tells us that there is one or more element under consideration for which the predicate is true***

**Example 7:** Determine the truth value of the statements ∃x (x > 3) and ∃x (x = x + 1).
