## Thm: (Pigeonhole Principle)

If $n$ objects are placed into $m$ containers and $n > m$, then at least one container holds more than one object.

---

## Cor:

A function $f : A \to B$ where $|A| = n$ and $|B| = m$ cannot be injective **if $n > m$**.

---

## Ex 1.

(a) In a room of 367 people, at least 2 of them will share a birthday.

- _Pigeons:_ 367 people. _Holes:_ 366 possible birthdays. Since $367 > 366$, at least two share a birthday.

(b) In a list of 27 English words, at least two of them will begin with the same letter.

- _Pigeons:_ 27 words. _Holes:_ 26 letters. Since $27 > 26$, at least two words share a first letter.

(c) How many students are needed in a class to be sure that at least two people get the same exam grade?

- _Holes:_ 101 possible grades (0–100). Need $101 + 1 = \boxed{102}$ students.

## Thm: (Generalized Pigeonhole Principle)

If there are $n$ objects and $k$ boxes, then there is at least one box with at least $\lceil \frac{n}{k} \rceil$ objects.

---

## Ex 2.

(a) Among 100 people, at least $\lceil 100/12 \rceil = \boxed{9}$ of them are born in the same month.

(b) A minimum of how many students are needed to ensure at least 6 people get the same letter grade?

- _Holes:_ 5 letter grades (A, B, C, D, F). Worst case: 5 students per grade. Need $5 \cdot 5 + 1 = \boxed{26}$ students.

(c) How many cards need to be drawn to ensure at least 3 from the same suit have been chosen?

- _Holes:_ 4 suits. Worst case: 2 cards per suit. Need $4 \cdot 2 + 1 = \boxed{9}$ cards.

(d) In a state with 25 million telephones, how many area codes are needed to ensure that every phone has a distinct phone number?

- Each area code supports $10^7$ phone numbers (7 digits). Need $\lceil 25{,}000{,}000 / 10{,}000{,}000 \rceil = \boxed{3}$ area codes.

## Ex 3.

Show that among any $n + 1$ positive integers not exceeding $2n$ there must be an integer that divides one of the other integers.

_Proof._ Write each integer $a_i$ in the form $a_i = 2^{k_i} \cdot q_i$ where $q_i$ is odd. Since each $a_i \leq 2n$, the odd part $q_i$ must be an odd integer in ${1, 3, 5, \ldots, 2n - 1}$, which contains exactly $n$ elements.

By the Pigeonhole Principle, among our $n + 1$ integers, at least two share the same odd part $q$. Say $a = 2^j \cdot q$ and $b = 2^k \cdot q$ with $j < k$. Then $a \mid b$. $\square$

## Thm: (Erdős–Szekeres)

Every sequence of $n^2 + 1$ distinct real numbers contains a subsequence of length $n + 1$ that is either strictly increasing or strictly decreasing.
