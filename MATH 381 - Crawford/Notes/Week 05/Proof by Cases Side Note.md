### Use cases:
- Proof by cases is typically used when you're stuck staring at a problem and thinking "I have no idea how to even start this."
- If you realize that what's true in one situation might not apply the same way in another situation, you probably need to split things up.
- Sometimes when you know you're dealing with a particular type of situation, you suddenly have extra information or tools that make the problem easier.
- **General problem:** "Prove all vehicles can transport people" (This feels overwhelming)
- **Specific cases:**
    - Cars → You can talk about engines, roads, seats
    - Boats → You can talk about water, floating, navigation
    - Planes → You can talk about wings, air pressure, flight

Each case gives you specific details that make the proof manageable.

## Real-World Analogy

Think of it like explaining why "everyone in your office building can get to the first floor":

- **If you're stuck on the general proof:** You might not know where to start
- **Breaking into cases helps:**
    - People on floors 2-10 → Use the elevator or stairs
    - People already on floor 1 → They're already there
    - People in the basement → Use the basement stairs

Each case gives you concrete, specific ways to approach the problem.

**Bottom line:** Use proof by cases when the general problem feels impossible, but specific situations give you the extra information you need to make progress.
