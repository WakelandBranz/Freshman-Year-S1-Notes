### The Principle of Strong Induction

The Principle of Strong Induction is based on the idea that if $P(b)$ is true (a Base Case) and if $P(b), P(b + 1), \cdots, P(k)$ implies $P(k + 1)$ (the Inductive Step), then $P(n)$ is true for all integers $n \geq b$.

I don't know how to climb all ladders, but I know how to climb some ladders.
I know how to climb a 5 rung ladder and a 3 rung ladder. Therefore I know how to climb an 8 rung ladder.

### Ex 1

Prove that every integer greater than 1 can be written as a prime or product of primes.

1. WTS every $n \in \mathbb{Z} \geq 2$ can be written as a prime or a product of primes.
2. Base case: $2 = 2$ which is prime so the base case is true.
3. Let $k \in \mathbb{Z} \geq 2$ be such that $k \geq 2$.
4. Inductive hypothesis: Assume that for all $2 \leq j \leq k$ for $j \in \mathbb{Z}$, $j$ can be written as a prime or a product of primes.
5. WTS $k + 1$ is a prime or is a product of primes.
Case 1: $k + 1$ is prime 
	Great! $k + 1$ is a prime
Case 2: $k + 1$ is composite (is not prime)
	By definition, $\exists a, b$ s.t. $a, b \geq 2$ and $a, b \leq k$, s.t. $k + 1 = a \cdot b$.
	Then by the inductive hypothesis, $a$ and $b$ are primes or a product of primes both respectively.
	Therefore $k + 1$ can be written as the product of the prodcuts of primes for $a$ and $b$ together, which is a product of primes.
	e.g. $$360 = 24 \cdot 15$$
	$$24 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3, 15 = 3 \cdot 5$$
	$$360 = (2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3) \cdot (3 \cdot 5)$$

### Ex 2

Prove that every amount of postage of 12-cents or more can be made using only 4 and 5-cent stamps.

1. Base case: $12 = 4(3)$ which is true
2. Let $k \geq 12 \in \mathbb{Z}$ be arbitrary.
3. Assume that $\forall j \in \mathbb{Z}$, $12 \leq j \leq k$, can be made with $4$ and $5$ cent stamps.
4. WTS $k + 1$ can be made with $4$ and $5$ cent stamps.

Case 1: There is at least $1$ $4$ cent stamp when we make $k$.
	Then we can change the 4 cent stamp to a 4 cent stamp to get $k + 1$.
Case 2: There are no 4 cent stamps when we make $k$.
	Then our number is divisible by $5$ so we can use $k / 5$ 5 cent stamps.
	Therefore $k + 1 \geq 15$, so we can use the same makeup for $k$, but replace 3 5 cent with 4 4 cents.


---

**Note:** In some situations for Strong Induction you may need more than one base case.
