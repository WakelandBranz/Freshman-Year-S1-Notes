**Def. Prime:** An integer $p > 1$ is called prime if the only positive divisors of $p$ are 1 and $p$ itself. An integer greater than 1 that is not prime is called composite.

This also means that $1$ is not a prime number. It has exactly one distinct positive divisor.

---

**Thm (Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic)** Every positive integer greater than 1 can be written uniquely as a prime or as a product of two or more primes in nondecreasing order.

---

**Ex 1.** Give the prime factorization of 100, 999, and 1024.

$100 = 2^2 \cdot 5^2$

$999 = 3^3 \cdot 37$

$1024 = 2^{10}$

**Thm** If $n$ is a composite integer, then $n$ has a prime divisor less than $\sqrt{n}$ (remember 1 is not prime).

**-PF-**

Assume that $n$ does not have at least one factor $\leq \sqrt{n}$, and $n$ is composite.
Take a factor of $n$, call it $a$, and its "counterpart", $\frac{n}{a}$.
But $a > \sqrt{n}$ and $\frac{n}{a} > \sqrt{ n } \to n = a \times \frac{n}{a} > (\sqrt{ n })^2 = n$.

**Def. Greatest Common Divisor:** The greatest common divisor of integers $a$ and $b$, not both zero, denoted $\gcd(a,b)$, is the largest positive integer that divides both $a$ and $b$.

---

**Def. Relatively prime:** Two integers $a$ and $b$ are relatively prime (or coprime) if $\gcd(a,b) = 1$.

---

**Def. Least Common Multiple:** The least common multiple of positive integers $a$ and $b$, denoted $\text{lcm}(a,b)$, is the smallest positive integer that is divisible by both $a$ and $b$.

---

**Thm** For positive integers $a$ and $b$, $\text{lcm}(a, b) \cdot \gcd(a, b) = a \cdot b$

---

**Ex 2.** Find $\gcd(91, 287)$

$91 = 7 \cdot 13$ and $287 = 7 \cdot 41$

Therefore, $\gcd(91, 287) = 7$

---

**Lemma** If $a = bq + r$ for integers $a, b, q, r$, then $\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, r)$.

---

**Ex 3.** Find $\gcd(414,662)$.

Using the Euclidean Algorithm:
$$\begin{align}
662 &= 414 \cdot 1 + 248 \\
414 &= 248 \cdot 1 + 166 \\
248 &= 166 \cdot 1 + 82 \\
166 &= 82 \cdot 2 + 2 \\
82 &= 2 \cdot 41 + 0
\end{align}$$

Therefore, $\gcd(414, 662) = 2$

---

**Thm (Bézout's Theorem)** If $a, b > 0$ are integers, then there exists integers $s$ and $t$ (called the Bézout Coefficients of $a$ and $b$) so that $\gcd(a,b) = sa + tb$.

---

**Ex 4.** Write the gcd of 252 and 198 as a linear combination of 252 and 198.

First, find $\gcd(252, 198)$ using the Euclidean Algorithm:
$$\begin{align}
252 &= 198 \cdot 1 + 54 \\
198 &= 54 \cdot 3 + 36 \\
54 &= 36 \cdot 1 + 18 \\
36 &= 18 \cdot 2 + 0
\end{align}$$

So $\gcd(252, 198) = 18$. Now work backwards:
$$\begin{align}
18 &= 54 - 36 \cdot 1 \\
&= 54 - (198 - 54 \cdot 3) \cdot 1 \\
&= 54 \cdot 4 - 198 \\
&= (252 - 198 \cdot 1) \cdot 4 - 198 \\
&= 252 \cdot 4 - 198 \cdot 5
\end{align}$$

Therefore, $18 = 4(252) + (-5)(198)$

**Lemma** If $a, b, c \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ so that $(a, b) = 1$ and $a \mid bc$, then $a \mid c$.

**Lemma** If $p$ is prime and $p \mid a_1 \cdots a_n$ (where each $a_i \in \mathbb{Z}$), then $p \mid a_j$ for some $j \in \{1, \cdots, n\}$.

**Thm** Each positive integer has a unique prime factorization.

---

**Thm** Let $m \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ and $a, b, c \in \mathbb{Z}$. If $ac \equiv bc \pmod{m}$, and $(c, m) = 1$, then $a \equiv b \pmod{m}$.
