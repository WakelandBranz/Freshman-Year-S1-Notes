## Overview

In most of our daily life, and in math, we do not argue as formally as we have done in the previous sections. Typically we do not so explicitly state when we use certain rules of inference or logical equivalences. Using these technical details would only muddle the ideas of an argument.

## Mathematical Terminology

### Key Definitions

**Theorem:** A statement that can be shown to be true. (usually important)
 - Theorems can also be referred to as facts or results.

**Proposition:** A less important theorem.

**Axioms:** Statements we assume to be true.
 - Used in proofs
 - AKA **postulates**

**Proof:** A valid argument showing a theorem is true

**Lemma:** A proposition usually used to prove a theorem.
 - Complicated proofs are usually easier to understand when they are proved using a series of lemmas, where each lemma is proved individually.

**Corollary:** A theorem that directly/immediately follows from another theorem.

**Conjecture:** A statement with supporting evidence that is thought to be true but has not been proven.
 - When proof of a **conjecture** is found, the **conjecture** becomes a theorem.

## Methods of Proofs

This section covers three basic approaches to a proof:

### 1. Direct Proof

**Definition** - When trying to prove the proposition $p \rightarrow q$, we assume p is true, use rules of inference, logical equivalences, axioms, t show q must be true.

**Even:** An integer `n` is even means $n = 2*k$, where `k` is an integer.

**Odd:** An integer `n` is odd means there exists an integer `k` such that $n = 2k + 1$.

**Perfect Square:** An integer n is a perfect square means $\exists$ an integer $k$ such that $k^2 = n$.

#### Examples

**Example 1:** Prove the statement "If n is an odd integer, then n² is also odd."

-**PF**-
Assume that n is odd.
Therefore, $\exists$ an integer k such that $n = 2k + 1$.
*Want to show $n^2$ is odd, as in, want to find some integer $L$ such that $n^2 = 2k + 1$.*
Note, $n^2 = (n)(n) = (2k + 1)(2k + 1) = 4k^2 + 4k + 1$.
Since $4k^2 + 4k + 1 = 2(2k^2 + 2k) + 1$.
Letting $L = (2k^2 + 2k)$, we see $n^2 = 2L + 1$ and $L$ is an integer (because k is an integer) so $n^2$ is odd.

**Example 2:** Prove "If n & m are both perfect squares then $(nm)$ is a perfect square."

**-MY PF-**
Assume that n & m are both perfect squares.
Therefore, $\exists$ an integer $k$ such that $n = k^2$ and $\exists$ an integer $L$ such that $m = L^2$.
Want to show that $(nm)$ is a perfect square, as in, want to find some integer $k$ and some integer $L$ such that $(nm) = (k^2L^2)$
Note, $n = (k)(k)$ and $m = (L)(L)$, thus $(nm) = (k^2L^2) = (k)(k)(L)(L)$.
Since both $k$ and $L$ are integers and since $k^2$ and $L^2$ are perfect squares, $(nm)$ is a perfect square.

**-MATT's PF-**
Assume n & m are perfect squares.
Then $\exists k, L \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $n = k^2$ and $m = L^2$. 
We want to show that $nm$ is a perfect square as in we want to show $\exists a \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $(nm) = a^2$.
Note $(k)(k) = n$ & $(L)(L) = m$, therefore $(nm) = n * m = (k)(k)(L)(L) = (k)(L)(k)(L)$.
Let $a = (k)(L)$.
Then, $(nm) = a^2$ and $a$ is an integer (b/c $k$ and $L$ are integers).

# Proof Methods Explained Simply

## **Direct Proof**

**What it is:** Start with what you know, then use logical steps to reach what you want to prove.

**How it works:** If you want to prove "If P, then Q," you assume P is true and show that Q must follow.

**Simple example:** Prove "If a number is even, then its square is even."

- Start: Assume n is even, so n = 2k for some integer k
- Work forward: n² = (2k)² = 4k² = 2(2k²)
- Conclusion: Since n² = 2(2k²), it's even ✓

**Think of it like:** Following a recipe step-by-step from ingredients to finished dish.

---

## **Proof by Contraposition**

**What it is:** Instead of proving "If P, then Q," prove "If not Q, then not P" (which is logically equivalent).

**How it works:** Assume Q is false and show that P must also be false.

**Simple example:** Prove "If n² is even, then n is even."

- Instead, prove: "If n is odd, then n² is odd"
- Start: Assume n is odd, so n = 2k + 1
- Work: n² = (2k + 1)² = 4k² + 4k + 1 = 2(2k² + 2k) + 1
- Conclusion: n² is odd, so our contrapositive is proven ✓

**Think of it like:** Proving "All cats are mammals" by showing "If it's not a mammal, it's not a cat."

---

## **Proof by Contradiction**

**What it is:** Assume the opposite of what you want to prove, then show this leads to something impossible.

**How it works:** Assume your statement is false, derive a contradiction, conclude your statement must be true.

**Simple example:** Prove "There are infinitely many prime numbers."

- Start: Assume there are only finitely many primes
- Work through the logic: If so, we could list them all and create a new number that's also prime
- Contradiction: This new prime wasn't in our "complete" list!
- Conclusion: Our assumption was wrong, so there must be infinitely many primes ✓

**Think of it like:** Proving someone is innocent by showing that assuming they're guilty leads to impossible conclusions.

The key difference: **Direct** goes straight forward, **contraposition** goes backward from the opposite conclusion, and **contradiction** assumes you're wrong and finds the impossibility.
