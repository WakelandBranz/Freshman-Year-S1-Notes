### Def. Equivalence Relation:

A relation $R$ on a set $A$ is an **equivalence relation** if it is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.

When $R$ is an equivalence relation, we often write $a \sim_R b$ or simply $a \sim b$ instead of $(a,b) \in R$.

---

### Ex 1.

Determine if each of the following are equivalence relations.

$R = \{(a, b) \mid a - b \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ on $A = \mathbb{R}$

$R = \{(a, b) \mid a|b\}$ on $A = \mathbb{Z}^+$

---

### Def. Equivalence Classes:

Let $R$ be an equivalence relation on a set $A$. For any element $a \in A$, the **equivalence class of $a$** is the set of all elements related to $a$:

$$[a]_R = \{b \in A \mid (a,b) \in R\} = \{b \in A \mid a \sim_R b\}$$

When the relation is clear from context, we may write $[a]$ instead of $[a]_R$.

The element $a$ is called a **representative** of the equivalence class $[a]_R$.

---

### Ex 2.

Let $R = \{(a, b) \mid a \equiv b \pmod{5}\}$ on $A = \mathbb{Z}$. Determine the equivalence classes of $R$.

---

### Thm

Let $R$ be an equivalence relation on $A$. For any $a, b \in A$, TFAE:

(i) $a \sim_R b$

(ii) $[a]_R = [b]_R$

(iii) $[a]_R \cap [b]_R \neq \emptyset$

---

### Def. Partition:

A **partition** of a set $S$ is a collection of nonempty subsets $\{A_i\}_{i \in I}$ of $S$ such that:

1. Each $A_i$ is nonempty: $A_i \neq \emptyset$ for all $i \in I$
2. The subsets are pairwise disjoint: $A_i \cap A_j = \emptyset$ for all $i \neq j$
3. The subsets cover $S$: $\bigcup_{i \in I} A_i = S$

In other words, every element of $S$ belongs to exactly one subset $A_i$.

---

### Ex 3.

Let $S = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$. Give a partition of $S$.

---

### Thm

Let $R$ be an equivalence relation on a set $S$. Then the equivalence classes of $R$ form a partition of $S$.

Conversely, given a partition $\{A_i\}_{i \in I}$ of a set $S$, there is an equivalence relation on $S$ with the sets $A_i$ as equivalence classes.
